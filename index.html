Abstract

Cross-species foundation models offer a potential solution to the scarcity of human intracortical recordings, yet their benefits remain inconsistent across decoding tasks. We investigate when nonhuman primate (NHP) motor recordings improve neural decoding and whether transfer depends on model capacity and target supervision. We introduce a masked neural reconstruction foundation model, a modular framework combining population tokenization, bidirectional masked reconstruction, and a sparse mixture of experts backbone. We compare pretraining on human speech motor recordings, a language associated cortical control, NHP motor recordings, and mixed human and NHP recordings. Across speech, handwriting, and reaching, models pretrained across species consistently outperform models trained from scratch. Furthermore, we show that cross species pretraining is capacity dependent, with its clearest benefits observed in smaller speech models and handwriting models adapted with intermediate amounts of labeled target data.

Method

MCFM tokenizes raw multi-channel neural activity into fixed-width patches, encodes them with a stack of blocks that route each sublayer's residual connection through a Sinkhorn-constrained hyper-connection instead of a plain skip, and pretrains the whole stack with a masked-reconstruction objective across five pretraining pools (A–E).

Key Findings

Cross-species pretraining provides conditional rather than universal benefits for intracortical decoding. Its strongest effect occurs for handwriting under restricted supervision, while speech depends on model capacity and NHP reaching primarily benefits from pretrained initialization rather than species mixing itself. These results establish task, capacity, supervision, and source composition as interacting design variables that must be reported jointly when evaluating cross-species neural foundation models.
